College football, current coaches

The coaches

Every head-coaching tenure of two or more seasons across the 21 programs on this site, current head coaches only: 20 tenures. Tenure years count seasons before the window and 2020.

Comparing coaches on wins alone rewards whoever inherited the best roster. Every chart here controls for that in some way. Play above roster is how well the team played (SP+) against what the recruiting rankings promised (247 talent composite), the cleanest single measure of coaching because it is opponent-adjusted and not driven by luck. Roster improvement is whether the coach raised the talent level from what he inherited. Inherited versus left is where the program stood when he arrived against where he left it. Wins against expectations and luck are shown but ranked last; at tenure sizes they are mostly noise.

Terms used on this page
Expected margin
How many points the team was expected to win or lose by, according to the point spread the sportsbooks posted just before kickoff. It sums up what oddsmakers, bettors, and the public expected, with money behind it, so this page uses it as the measure of expectations. A week-earlier version (the opening number) also exists; when the two differ, opinion moved during the week.
Expected wins
Each game's expected margin converted to a chance of winning, then added up. The record the public thought was coming.
Deserved wins
Each game's postgame win probability, from CollegeFootballData's play-by-play model, added up. How many games a team that played that way usually wins. The gap between actual and deserved wins is what most people call luck.
SP+
Bill Connelly's efficiency rating of how well a team actually played, adjusted for opponent. Used here as the measure of quality, separate from the record.
Talent composite
247Sports' rating of the whole roster's recruiting pedigree. Used here as the measure of what the players were supposed to be.
Beat expectations (by)
Won by more than the expected margin, or lost by less. "Beat by" is the actual margin minus the expected margin: +7 means seven points better than expected. A bettor would say the team covered.
One-score game
Decided by eight points or fewer.
Head coach, tenure year
The head coach who worked the most games that season, and how many seasons into his run at the school it was, counting seasons before this page's window and the 2020 season.
Standard error
The size of gap that random variation alone produces about a third of the time. A number within one standard error is noise. Beyond two, it is probably real. Each takeaway has a "How sure is this?" note that says which.

Recruiting against developing

One dot per tenure. Left to right is roster improvement: the talent rank two seasons before the coach arrived minus the talent rank from his third season on. Up and down is play above roster, measured against the field average so the middle line is "typical for these programs". Larger dots are longer tenures. Dots on the edge are off the chart in that direction; hover for the number.

Takeaway: Recruits and develops: nobody. Recruits but does not develop: nobody. Develops what he gets: Cignetti (Indiana). Neither: nobody. Everyone else sits near the middle on at least one axis. Dot size grows with seasons coached.

How sure is this?

Roster improvement compares the two seasons before the coach arrived with his third season on, so it is blank for tenures that never reached year three and marked with an asterisk in the table where a shorter span had to stand in. One standard error on a rank average is roughly ±11 spots divided by the square root of the seasons: ±8 for two seasons, ±5 for five, ±4 for nine. The portal era lets a roster improve without the recruiting rank moving, which understates coaches who build through transfers.

Play above roster, ranked

Talent rank minus SP+ rank across the tenure, with one standard error either side. The dashed line is the field average; the talent composite rates every elite roster higher than it plays, so that line, not zero, is the fair bar.

Takeaway: Clearly above the field average, error bar included: Cignetti (Indiana, +58), Kiffin (Ole Miss, +12), Heupel (Tennessee, +6), Day (Ohio St, +1). Clearly below it: Lebby (Miss St, -44), Napier (Florida, -27), Beamer (S Carolina, -21), Lea (Vanderbilt, -20), Freeze (Auburn, -16), Stoops (Kentucky, -15). 10 tenures overlap the average.

How sure is this?

The whisker on each dot is one standard error. Short tenures have long whiskers by construction, and fired coaches have short tenures by construction, so the bottom of the chart skews toward the recently fired; read it as "what the evidence supports so far", not a final grade.

Inherited versus left

Where the program stood in SP+ in the two seasons before the coach arrived (open circle) and in his last two seasons, or latest two if he is still there (filled dot). Best is to the left. Sorted by improvement.

Takeaway: Biggest turnarounds: Cignetti (Indiana, No. 91 to No. 6), Lea (Vanderbilt, No. 114 to No. 30), Kiffin (Ole Miss, No. 42 to No. 4). Biggest declines: Lebby (Miss St, No. 40 to No. 73), Napier (Florida, No. 18 to No. 42), Moore (Michigan, No. 2 to No. 26). Open circle is the program's SP+ rank in the two seasons before the coach arrived; filled dot is his last two seasons, or his latest two if he is still there.

How sure is this?

Two-season averages on both ends carry about ±8 spots each, so a swing under about 15 spots is inside the noise. A coach who inherited a top-five program cannot show a turnaround no matter how well he coached, which is why this chart is paired with the play-above-roster chart rather than used alone.
