College football, 2023–2025

The SEC and the national field

The sixteen SEC programs plus Clemson, Indiana, Michigan, Notre Dame and Ohio State: every recent national champion and every program whose average final AP ranking over 2016–2025 sits inside the top 12. Texas and Oklahoma's Big 12 seasons are included so each program has the same window. 829 team-games over 3 seasons, every one with an expected margin.

Every season that goes differently than expected comes down to one of three things: the expectations were wrong, the bounces were wrong, or the development was wrong. This page asks those three questions of the sixteen SEC programs and 5 of the country's elite at once, then of every head-coaching tenure, then asks whether any of it changes as a season goes on. Each chart ends with a short takeaway, with a note on how much to trust it. 3 seasons, 2023 through 2025: about 39 games per team. Every team also has its own page in the menu above.

Terms used on this page
Expected margin
How many points the team was expected to win or lose by, according to the point spread the sportsbooks posted just before kickoff. It sums up what oddsmakers, bettors, and the public expected, with money behind it, so this page uses it as the measure of expectations. A week-earlier version (the opening number) also exists; when the two differ, opinion moved during the week.
Expected wins
Each game's expected margin converted to a chance of winning, then added up. The record the public thought was coming.
Deserved wins
Each game's postgame win probability, from CollegeFootballData's play-by-play model, added up. How many games a team that played that way usually wins. The gap between actual and deserved wins is what most people call luck.
SP+
Bill Connelly's efficiency rating of how well a team actually played, adjusted for opponent. Used here as the measure of quality, separate from the record.
Talent composite
247Sports' rating of the whole roster's recruiting pedigree. Used here as the measure of what the players were supposed to be.
Beat expectations (by)
Won by more than the expected margin, or lost by less. "Beat by" is the actual margin minus the expected margin: +7 means seven points better than expected. A bettor would say the team covered.
One-score game
Decided by eight points or fewer.
Head coach, tenure year
The head coach who worked the most games that season, and how many seasons into his run at the school it was, counting seasons before this page's window and the 2020 season.
Standard error
The size of gap that random variation alone produces about a third of the time. A number within one standard error is noise. Beyond two, it is probably real. Each takeaway has a "How sure is this?" note that says which.

Overrated, underrated, lucky, unlucky

Up and down is what the public expected minus what the play deserved: up is overrated. Left and right is actual wins minus deserved wins: right is overperforming, usually close games and turnover luck. 3-season totals. A team can be overrated and unlucky at the same time; that is the top-left corner.

Takeaway: By this measure LSU is the most overrated program on this site (+2.8 wins) and Missouri the most underrated (-3.1). Georgia has won the most beyond what its play deserved (+4.3) and Arkansas the least (-4.4). Texas A&M sits at -0.3 on the expectation axis and -1.6 on the luck axis: expected about right, neither lucky nor unlucky. The field as a whole clusters near the middle; the corners are a handful of programs.

How sure is this?

One standard error is about ±1.9 wins up and down and ±1.7 left and right. Beyond two standard errors on either axis: Arkansas, Georgia. Between one and two: Alabama, Auburn, LSU, Mississippi State, Missouri, Oklahoma, Ole Miss, Texas, Vanderbilt, Clemson, Indiana, Michigan. Everyone else is inside the noise, and the luck axis in particular needs many seasons to say much.
