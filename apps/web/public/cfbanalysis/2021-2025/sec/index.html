Every season that goes differently than expected comes down to one of three things: the expectations were wrong, the bounces were wrong, or the development was wrong. This page asks those three questions of all sixteen programs at once, then of every head-coaching tenure, then asks whether any of it changes as a season goes on. Each chart ends with a short takeaway, with a note on how much to trust it. 5 seasons, 2021 through 2025: about 66 games per team. Every team also has its own page in the menu above.

Terms used on this page
Expected margin
How many points the team was expected to win or lose by, according to the point spread the sportsbooks posted just before kickoff. It sums up what oddsmakers, bettors, and the public expected, with money behind it, so this page uses it as the measure of expectations. A week-earlier version (the opening number) also exists; when the two differ, opinion moved during the week.
Expected wins
Each game's expected margin converted to a chance of winning, then added up. The record the public thought was coming.
Deserved wins
Each game's postgame win probability, from CollegeFootballData's play-by-play model, added up. How many games a team that played that way usually wins. The gap between actual and deserved wins is what most people call luck.
SP+
Bill Connelly's efficiency rating of how well a team actually played, adjusted for opponent. Used here as the measure of quality, separate from the record.
Talent composite
247Sports' rating of the whole roster's recruiting pedigree. Used here as the measure of what the players were supposed to be.
Beat expectations (by)
Won by more than the expected margin, or lost by less. "Beat by" is the actual margin minus the expected margin: +7 means seven points better than expected. A bettor would say the team covered.
One-score game
Decided by eight points or fewer.
Head coach, tenure year
The head coach who worked the most games that season, and how many seasons into his run at the school it was, counting seasons before this page's window and the 2020 season.
Standard error
The size of gap that random variation alone produces about a third of the time. A number within one standard error is noise. Beyond two, it is probably real. Each takeaway has a "How sure is this?" note that says which.

Overrated, underrated, lucky, unlucky

Up and down is what the public expected minus what the play deserved: up is overrated. Left and right is actual wins minus deserved wins: right is overperforming, usually close games and turnover luck. 5-season totals. A team can be overrated and unlucky at the same time; that is the top-left corner.

Takeaway: By this measure Florida is the most overrated program in the league (+4.1 wins) and South Carolina the most underrated (-3.4). Georgia has won the most beyond what its play deserved (+5.4) and Auburn the least (-5.5). Texas A&M sits at +2.6 on the expectation axis and -1.0 on the luck axis: leaning overrated, neither lucky nor unlucky. The league as a whole clusters near the middle; the corners are a handful of programs.

How sure is this?

One standard error is about ±2.5 wins up and down and ±2.3 left and right. Beyond two standard errors on either axis: Arkansas, Auburn, Georgia. Between one and two: Alabama, Florida, LSU, Mississippi State, Missouri, Oklahoma, Ole Miss, South Carolina, Texas A&M. Everyone else is inside the noise, and the luck axis in particular needs many seasons to say much.

Beat expectations by, on average

Positive means the program beat its expected margin on average; negative means the public was consistently too optimistic about it.

Takeaway: Over 5 seasons there is no SEC team whose expected margins are reliably off. The most optimistic the public has been about any program is 2.7 points a game (Florida), the most pessimistic 2.8 (South Carolina), and the whole league spans about 5 points. That is what you would expect when the number is set by people with money on it.

How sure is this?

With about 66 games per team, one standard error on a beat-expectations rate is roughly ±6 points. Outside two standard errors: no team.
